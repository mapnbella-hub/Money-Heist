Where the Magic Happened

Filming Locations of Money Heist

Spain and Beyond

While Money Heist is set primarily in Madrid, the series was filmed across multiple locations in Spain and Italy. From the iconic Royal Mint of Spain to the hidden villa in Toledo, each location was carefully chosen to bring the story to life. The production team transformed real buildings into the sets we see on screen, blending practical locations with studio work.

Key Locations

Main Places

Royal Mint of Spain (Fábrica Nacional de Moneda y Timbre)

Location: Madrid, Spain

The main setting for Parts 1 & 2. The actual building is located in the Salamanca district of Madrid. Exteriors were filmed at the real Mint, while interiors were recreated in studios.

Primary Location

Bank of Spain (Banco de España)

Location: Madrid, Spain

The central setting for Parts 3-5. The Bank of Spain is one of Madrid's most iconic buildings, located in the city center. The gold vault scenes were filmed on specially built sets.

Primary Location

Beyond Madrid

Other Filming Locations

Toledo, Spain

The historic city of Toledo served as the location for the Hacienda — the villa where the Professor planned the heist and where the team stayed before the Royal Mint heist. The winding streets and ancient architecture provided a perfect backdrop.

Hacienda & Training Ground
Florence, Italy

Florence and Tuscany were featured prominently in flashback scenes showing the Professor and Berlin planning the heist. The Italian countryside represented freedom and escape, contrasting with the claustrophobic interiors of the Mint and Bank.

Planning & Flashback Scenes
Madrid City, Spain

Various neighborhoods in Madrid were used for exterior shots: the Salamanca district for the Royal Mint, the Gran Vía for chase scenes, and Plaza de Cibeles for key moments. The city itself became a character in the story.

The Soul of the Series

Spain's Influence on the Show

🇪🇸 SPAIN

Spanish Identity

The series is deeply rooted in Spanish culture. From the language to the music, from the locations to the social commentary — Money Heist is unmistakably Spanish. The show reflects Spain's complex history, its economic struggles, and the spirit of its people. The red and gold color palette of the series directly mirrors the Spanish flag, and the themes of resistance echo Spain's historical narratives.

Spanish Music & Culture

Beyond "Bella Ciao," the series features Spanish music, flamenco influences, and traditional elements woven into its soundtrack. The characters' names (city names from around the world) contrast with their deeply Spanish identities, creating a unique cultural blend that resonated globally.
